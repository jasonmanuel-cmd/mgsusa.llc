AT A GLANCE

The best glass-cleaning routine is a short, regular one.

A squeegee after each shower keeps soap scum and hard water from setting in. For deeper cleaning, a vinegar-and-water mix or a non-abrasive glass cleaner does the job without damaging the glass or the hardware finish. Avoid abrasive scrubbers and harsh chemicals — they can etch the glass and wear the hardware. For storefronts and commercial glass, a clean, lint-free cloth and a standard glass cleaner are usually enough for daily maintenance.

WHEN TO START HERE

When to start here.

  • Your shower glass has soap scum, hard water spots, or a cloudy film that a regular wipe-down will not remove.
  • You want to keep newly installed glass looking clean without damaging it.
  • Your storefront or window glass has streaks, spots, or buildup that needs a proper clean.
  • You are trying to figure out whether cloudy glass is dirt, hard water, or permanent etching.
  • You want to know what products and tools are safe for tempered, laminated, and coated glass.

HOW THE ROUTINE WORKS

A clearer next step.

  1. 01

    Daily or after each use — squeegee

    For shower glass, a quick squeegee after each use prevents soap scum and mineral buildup from adhering. It takes seconds and is the single biggest factor in how long glass stays clean.

  2. 02

    Weekly — gentle wash

    Wash with a mixture of warm water and a few drops of dish soap or a non-abrasive glass cleaner. Use a soft cloth or non-abrasive sponge. Rinse and dry with a lint-free cloth to avoid streaks.

  3. 03

    For hard water stains — vinegar solution

    A mixture of white vinegar and water (about 1:1) applied with a soft cloth, left to sit briefly, then rinsed and dried, removes most hard water deposits. Repeat as needed for heavier buildup. Avoid vinegar on natural stone surrounds — it can etch stone.

  4. 04

    What to avoid

    Do not use abrasive scrubbers, steel wool, or harsh acidic or alkaline cleaners on glass. Do not use razor blades on coated or tinted glass unless you know the coating can take it. Avoid cleaners that leave a film — they attract more buildup over time.

When cleaning is not enough

Cloudy, crazed, or permanently etched glass is a replacement problem, not a cleaning problem.

Hard water stains and soap scum come off with the right cleaning. Permanent etching, crazing, and seal failure on insulated glass units do not — they require replacement. How do I clean a frameless shower enclosure?+

Squeegee after each use to prevent soap scum and hard water buildup. For a deeper clean, use a mixture of warm water and a few drops of dish soap or a non-abrasive glass cleaner. Apply with a soft cloth or non-abrasive sponge, rinse, and dry with a lint-free cloth. For hard water stains, a 1:1 white vinegar and water solution applied with a soft cloth, left to sit briefly, then rinsed and dried, removes most deposits. Avoid abrasive scrubbers and harsh chemicals — they can damage the glass and the hardware finish.

What removes hard water stains from glass?+

A 1:1 mixture of white vinegar and water applied with a soft cloth, left to sit for a few minutes, then rinsed and dried, removes most hard water stains. For heavier buildup, repeat the process or let the solution sit longer. For glass with a protective coating, check the manufacturer's cleaning recommendations first — some coatings do not tolerate vinegar. Avoid vinegar on natural stone surrounds such as marble or travertine.

Can I use a razor blade to clean glass?+

A fresh razor blade held at a shallow angle can remove stubborn deposits from bare, uncoated glass — but it is not safe on coated, tinted, or laminated glass unless you know the coating can take it. For most residential cleaning, a non-abrasive sponge and the right cleaner do the job without the risk. If in doubt, skip the blade and use a dedicated glass-cleaning product.

Why does my shower glass go cloudy so fast?+

Soap scum and hard water minerals build up quickly on glass in areas with hard water — and much of the Texas Hill Country has hard water. A squeegee after each use is the single biggest factor in how long glass stays clean. Without it, the buildup bonds to the glass and becomes harder to remove over time. Once the buildup has etched into the surface, it may not come off with cleaning alone — at that point replacement is the right answer.

How do I clean storefront glass without streaks?+

Use a clean, lint-free cloth or microfiber towel and a standard glass cleaner or a water-and-dish-soap mixture. Clean from top to bottom in overlapping strokes. Dry with a second clean lint-free cloth to avoid streaks. Clean in shade or during cooler parts of the day — direct sun dries the cleaner too fast and causes streaks. For large storefronts, a squeegee followed by a dry cloth works well.

When should I replace glass instead of cleaning it?+

Replace glass when it is permanently etched, crazed, or scratched to the point that cleaning no longer improves it. Replace double-pane windows when the seal has failed and you see fogging or condensation between the panes — the insulating gas is gone and the seal cannot be repaired in the field. Replace glass that is cracked, chipped, or has failed — safety and security come first.
