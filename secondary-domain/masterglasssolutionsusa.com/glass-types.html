AT A GLANCE

Glass types are not interchangeable.

Tempered glass is required for safety-critical openings like shower doors and sidelites. Laminated glass holds together when broken — useful for storefronts, overhead glazing, and security. Insulated and low-E glass manage heat gain and loss. The right choice is a property-level decision, not a one-size-fits-all answer.

WHEN YOU MAY NEED THIS

When to start here.

  • You are choosing glass for a new shower enclosure, storefront, window, or custom opening.
  • A current glass type is causing heat gain, energy loss, condensation, or noise issues.
  • A building inspector, architect, or code requirement specifies a particular glass type.
  • You want to understand why one glass costs more than another before requesting a quote.
  • You are comparing tempered vs. laminated for a door, sidelite, or overhead installation.

What influences the investment?

Glass type, thickness, size, edge work, and installation access all shape cost.

Tempered glass is the baseline for most residential and commercial work. Laminated and insulated glass cost more because of the manufacturing process. Low-E coatings add a modest premium. Larger pieces, custom sizes, and thicker stock also move the price. A photo and rough dimensions are often enough to start the conversation.

What is the difference between tempered and laminated glass?+

Tempered glass is heat-treated to be significantly stronger than annealed glass and shatters into small, blunt pieces when broken — that is why building codes require it for showers, doors, and sidelites. Laminated glass has a plastic interlayer between two glass plies; when broken, the interlayer holds the pieces in place. Laminated glass is used where retention of the glass is important — overhead glazing, security applications, and some storefront systems.

Is tempered glass required by code for showers?+

Yes. Shower and tub enclosure glass must be tempered or otherwise meet safety-glass requirements. Master Glass Solutions uses tempered glass for all shower enclosures, with 3/8-inch or thicker stock for frameless designs.

What does low-E glass do?+

Low-E (low-emissivity) glass has a thin metallic coating that reflects infrared heat. In hot climates like San Antonio, a low-E coating on the outer surface helps keep heat out. In heated buildings, it reflects interior heat back inside. Low-E is a choice for energy-conscious window and storefront installations.

When is insulated glass the right choice?+

Insulated glass units (IGUs) have two or more glass plies separated by a sealed air or gas space. They reduce heat transfer and condensation compared to single-pane glass. IGUs are common in window replacements and storefront systems where energy performance and condensation control matter.

Does Master Glass Solutions handle the glass specification?+

Yes. When you request a quote, we identify the appropriate glass type, thickness, and edge finish for the opening. For code-required locations like showers and doors, we confirm the safety-glass requirement before ordering. Call 210-370-3700 with the opening details, or use the quote form.
