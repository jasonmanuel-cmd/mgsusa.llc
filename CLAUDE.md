# Master Glass Solutions — mgsusa.llc

Static HTML marketing site for a San Antonio, TX glass company, deployed on
Vercel with a handful of Node serverless functions in `api/`. **No framework, no
build step, no bundler** — pages are hand-edited HTML, JS is vanilla, served as-is.
The one build script (`npm run build:vendor`) only re-bundles the Vercel Blob
client into `assets/vendor/blob-client.js` with esbuild.

## Layout

- `*.html` (67 pages) at the repo root — services, ~25 `<city>-tx.html` local
  landing pages, legal pages, `followup-desk.html`, `review.html`. The three
  commercial-film pages (`commercial-security-film.html`,
  `commercial-window-tint.html`, `perforated-window-graphics.html`) are
  commercial-building only by the owner's instruction — no auto glass — and
  were generated by `tools/build_film_pages.py`, wired into the nav and
  sitemap by `tools/wire_film_pages.py`, given the home-page CTA by
  `tools/add_film_cta.py` and their explainer diagrams by
  `tools/add_film_diagrams.py`. Edit them through those scripts.
  **`build_film_pages.py --apply` is not safe to re-run.** It rebuilds `<main>`
  from the donor, so it discards whatever a later script appended — re-running
  it on 2026-10-20 silently deleted all three explainer diagrams, and the page
  looked completely normal afterwards. If you must regenerate, re-run
  `add_film_diagrams.py --apply` straight after, and diff before committing.
- `glass-railings.html` — built by `tools/build_railings_page.py`, wired by
  `tools/wire_railings_page.py`. The generator imports
  `build_film_pages.py`'s section builders rather than copying them; the one
  thing it overrides is the CTA target, via `quote_url()`, because railings are
  residential as often as commercial and a homeowner sent to
  `/commercial-quote` is being asked for property-management details they do
  not have. The same no-re-run warning applies.
  **Correction, 2026-10-21: this file previously said there were no photographs
  of this company's glass railings. There are four.** `Stairs1`–`Stairs4` are
  frameless glass stair railings, already captioned "Glass railings" and
  already live on `gallery.html` and `residential-glass.html`. They were missed
  because they are named `Stairs*`, so a search for railing photos found
  nothing, and the page about glass railings led with a commercial storefront
  while four railing photos sat in `assets/`. The hero is `Stairs1` now.
  The lesson is the naming: **search the gallery captions, not the filenames**
  — the captions say what a photo shows, the filename says which folder it
  arrived in. The slideshow image captioned "Balcony rail and window glazing
  detail" really is a *metal* picket rail, which is what sent the first search
  down the wrong path.
- **City pages** — 28 `<city>-tx.html`. The seven newest (`stone-oak-tx`,
  `alamo-ranch-tx`, `bulverde-tx`, `fair-oaks-ranch-tx`, `live-oak-tx`,
  `selma-tx`, `garden-ridge-tx`) are built by
  `tools/build_city_pages.py` and wired by `tools/wire_city_pages.py`; the
  older 21 came from `deepen_city_pages.py` and its sibling. They exist
  because the page set was aimed at the wrong towns: lining the Google
  Business Profile's service areas against the city pages showed seven profile
  areas with no page — all seven now have one — and eight pages for towns the
  profile did not claim, and
  **every town in the first group is closer to the office than every town in
  the second.** Stone Oak is 5.5 miles out, affluent and dense, and had no
  page; Stockdale is 41 miles out with under 1,500 people and had one.
  The content on each is authored, not substituted. A city page made by
  swapping one town name for another is a duplicate with extra steps, which is
  why `tools/content_uniqueness.py` exists — the older 21 average ~41% unique
  and the seven new ones score 59–64%, the top seven rows of the table. Keep it that way:
  each page is built around something that genuinely changes the glass work
  there (hard water etching, failed 1970s–90s sealed units, acreage access,
  west-facing afternoon sun, 1960s aluminium glazing, retail tenant turnover,
  falling oak limbs), never around invented jobs, customers or population
  figures. `garden-ridge-tx` is the one that argues *against* a product: under
  heavy oak canopy the solar gain that sells film elsewhere is already handled,
  so the page says so rather than selling film to a shaded window.
  Two traps the generator fell into, both now asserted against in `check()`:
  the nearby-areas markup is `.nearby-grid > a.nearby-city`, **not** the
  `.related-grid` pattern the service pages use, and a non-matching regex is
  not an error — the first run silently kept the donor's neighbours, 20 to 36
  miles away on the wrong side of town. And the hero eyebrow carries the town
  name in **literal capitals**, so a title-case replace left "GLASS SERVICES IN
  TIMBERWOOD PARK, TX" above the h1 of a page about Stone Oak. Every automated
  check passed; it was caught by looking at a screenshot.
- `api/` — serverless functions (see table below).
- `data/` — UMD modules shared by browser and functions: exposed as `window.MGS.*`
  in the browser, `module.exports` on Vercel. Includes the followup store/auth helpers
  and `metrics-cache.js` (server-only Blob TTL cache for dashboard reads).
- `assets/js/`, `assets/css/` — per-feature client code and styles.
  `slideshow.js` drives the project carousel that sits under the hero on every
  service page.
- `service-worker.js` — cache-first for images, network-first for HTML, CSS and JS.
  Registered by `assets/js/sw-register.js` on all 65 real pages, placed by
  `tools/add_sw_register.py`. Until 2026-10-20 registration was an inline
  snippet on 7 pages only — index, contact, the two glass hubs and the three
  quote pages — so the other 53, every city landing page among them, never
  installed the worker. The visitor arriving from a local search was the one
  least likely to get the caching this file exists to provide, while the
  cache-version rule below was the most-repeated instruction in this document.
  The registration is deliberately silent and swallows its own failures: the
  site is perfectly usable with no worker, so a caching optimisation must
  never be able to surface an error on a page.
- `.claude/skills/add-project-photos/` — the photo pipeline as a runnable skill,
  with `optimize_images.py` and `verify_site.py` in its `scripts/`.
- `vercel.json` — `cleanUrls`, `.html` → extensionless redirects for every page,
  and per-endpoint `Cache-Control` headers.
- `llms.txt` is hand-maintained; **`llms-full.txt` is generated** by
  `tools/build_llms_full.py` and must not be hand-edited below its Scope of
  Work section. The generator preserves the hand-authored preamble (title
  block, Quick Answers, Scope of Work) verbatim and rebuilds everything after
  it from `<main>` on each page, so a new page reaches it by re-running rather
  than by someone remembering. It had covered 5 of 60 pages and still quoted
  the corrected review count when this was written. Two deliberate choices:
  the 21 city pages are summarised as a link index rather than dumped, because
  111 KB of near-identical prose crowds out distinct content in a retrieval
  index, and only `<main>` is read, because the previous file began almost
  every section with the nav and so embedded the same boilerplate 5 times.
  It is idempotent — running it twice gives the same bytes.

## Serverless endpoints

| Endpoint | Purpose |
|---|---|
| `api/chat.js` | Project Assistant chat; **OpenRouter** provider (was OpenAI), grounded in `data/company-knowledge.js`. **No Turnstile** — it was removed after the widget failed to load and 403'd every message; guarded instead by a per-IP rate limit (10/min, 60/hr, in-lambda memory) |
| `api/submit-quote.js` | Quote form: validate → Blob photo upload → Resend lead email |
| `api/blob-upload.js` | Single-photo client upload token handler |
| `api/google-reviews.js` | Google **Places API (New)** proxy; server-side key only |
| `api/reviews-fallback.js` | Static empty-review payload, same shape |
| `api/followup-desk-login.js` | Passcode → HMAC session token |
| `api/followup-add.js` | Auth'd: save customer, send satisfaction email |
| `api/followup-list.js` | Auth'd: list customer records (pure read, cache disabled). The desk's "Export customer list" tile turns this into a CSV client-side — quoted, formula-injection guarded, BOM for Excel, and deliberately without the review token |
| `api/review-submit.js` | Public: 4–5★ → Google review link email; 1–3★ → private owner alert |
| `api/health-check.js` | Daily Vercel cron (14:00 UTC). Three checks: POSTs a tokenless synthetic quote to the live `/api/submit-quote` in probe mode, asks Resend whether the `LEAD_FROM_EMAIL` domain is verified, and flags a lead drought. Silent when healthy; emails the owner only on failure, falling back to `onboarding@resend.dev` when the normal sender is the thing that broke. Guarded by `CRON_SECRET` |
| `api/site-metrics.js` | Auth'd: dashboard aggregator — follow-up funnel, Google rating, GA4 traffic, Lighthouse/PSI, live site probe. `maxDuration: 60` (a PSI run takes 10-30s) |

All write/auth endpoints get `Cache-Control: no-store` in `vercel.json`; the
generic `/api/(.*)` rule caches for 6h with a 24h stale-while-revalidate window.

## Environment variables (Vercel)

`OPENROUTER_API_KEY`, `OPENROUTER_MODEL`, `OPENAI_API_KEY`, `OPENAI_MODEL`,
`RESEND_API_KEY`, `LEAD_FROM_EMAIL`, `LEAD_NOTIFICATION_EMAIL` (accepts a
comma- or semicolon-separated list; see `data/lead-recipients.js`),
`BLOB_READ_WRITE_TOKEN`, `FOLLOWUP_BLOB_READ_WRITE_TOKEN` (dedicated store),
`FOLLOWUP_DESK_PASSCODE`, `FOLLOWUP_SESSION_SECRET`, `TURNSTILE_SECRET_KEY`,
`GOOGLE_PLACES_API_KEY`, `GOOGLE_PLACE_ID`, `GOOGLE_REVIEW_URL`, `GOOGLE_MAPS_URL`.

Monitoring, all optional — the cron degrades rather than failing: `CRON_SECRET`
(sent by the Vercel cron as a bearer token; unset means the endpoint is open,
fine for preview but not production), `SITE_URL` (default
`https://www.mgsusa.llc`), `LEAD_DROUGHT_DAYS` (default 30),
`LEAD_DROUGHT_REPEAT_DAYS` (default 30), `HEALTH_PROBE_EMAIL`
(an address that is safe to receive a test message; setting it makes the
funnel probe perform a real Resend send instead of only reporting that a key
exists — the difference between "configured" and "actually delivers"),
`HEALTH_PROBE_SEND_DAYS` (default 7; how often that real send happens).

**Not every failing check should email.** `health-check` keeps "this check
failed" and "this is worth an email" apart, and a check opts out by returning
`notify: false`. The funnel and sending-domain checks mean customers are being
turned away *right now*, so they mail on every failing run until fixed. A lead
drought does not: at ~100 visitors a week a fortnight of silence is usually
just a quiet fortnight, and it was mailing the owner daily for 18 days running.
It now mails on the day it crosses the threshold and every
`LEAD_DROUGHT_REPEAT_DAYS` after, while the endpoint's JSON still reports it
every time (the suppressed ones are listed under `suppressed`). The reason is
the promise the endpoint makes — an arriving message always means something
needs attention. A daily alert nobody can act on spends that promise, and it
spends it on the same subject line that carries "customers cannot submit a
quote right now".

**The same rule applies to a check that succeeds.** With `HEALTH_PROBE_EMAIL`
set, the probe's real Resend send put `[probe] quote funnel check` in the
owner's inbox every single morning — a success report, from the same sender,
in the same mailbox, as "customers cannot submit a quote right now". That is
the lead-drought mistake with the sign flipped: one alerted daily on something
nobody could act on, the other reported daily on something nobody needed to
know. The **tokenless POST still runs every day** — that is the detector, and
throttling it would delete the thing this endpoint exists for — but the real
send is now weekly (`HEALTH_PROBE_SEND_DAYS`, default 7, Thursdays; set it to
1 for the old behaviour). The daily sending-domain check already covers the
failure the send was mainly guarding, at the cost of one GET and no mail;
the send covers what that cannot see — a revoked or over-quota key, a
suppression entry, Resend down — and those do not need proving twice a week.
Scheduling is stateless, derived from the day number exactly as the drought's
is, so there is nothing stored to fall out of step.

The threshold itself went 7 → 30 days for the same reason. Seven was picked
before anyone measured how often a lead actually arrives here, and they arrive
weeks apart even when every check is green — so the business sat below the
threshold most of the time and the check reported the weather rather than
detecting anything. This does mean the drought would have caught the August
outage later than a 7-day threshold would. That is acceptable only because the
drought is no longer the detector: the funnel probe exercises the live endpoint
daily and mails the moment a tokenless quote is refused, and with
`HEALTH_PROBE_EMAIL` set it proves the mail leaves. The drought is the backstop
behind those, and a backstop should be slow and quiet, or it drowns out the
detectors in front of it.

Dashboard-only, all optional — the matching card shows a setup hint instead of data:
`GA4_PROPERTY_ID`, `GA4_CLIENT_EMAIL`, `GA4_PRIVATE_KEY` (service account with
Viewer on the GA4 property; the PEM goes in with `\n` escapes) and
`PAGESPEED_API_KEY` (PSI works unkeyed but is rate limited).

The Turnstile **site** key is inlined in `assets/js/quote-form.js` and
`assets/js/review-page.js` — change it in both when it rotates. Secrets stay in
Vercel. `TURNSTILE_SECRET_KEY` is still verified by `submit-quote` and
`blob-upload`; the chat no longer uses it.

The quote form and review page share the site key
`0x4AAAAAAEGumU2z9QHnLmlL`, the same one whose widget failure broke the chat.
That key failing no longer costs a lead: both pages had their blocking gate
removed (see the comment at `quote-form.js:564`), so a missing token now rides
along as absent and the server treats the lead as unverified rather than
refusing it. Still worth keeping the allowed hostnames correct in the
Cloudflare dashboard — an unverified lead arrives with an `[unverified]`
subject prefix, which is noise, not loss.

(This paragraph used to say the form "blocks submission when no token is
minted, so the quote form is silently dropping leads". That was true when it
was written and was fixed weeks before this correction. It is called out
rather than quietly deleted because a stale warning about a fixed bug is

## Conventions

- **CSS:** edit `assets/design-tokens.css` (source of truth) and
  `assets/styles.css`, then regenerate `assets/styles.min.css` with
  `tools/build_styles_min.py --apply` — that's what pages actually load. Never
  patch the `.min` file directly. **Until 2026-10-24 that tool did not exist**,
  so the only way to obey this instruction was to hand-edit both files and hope
  they stayed in step; they happened to, which is luck, not process. The
  difference between the two files is comment-stripping and whitespace, nothing
  more. Two things the tool is careful about, both learned by getting them
  wrong: **declaration values are copied through verbatim**, because a custom
  property keeps its token stream as written (`--font-sans:Inter,ui-sans-serif`
  and `--font-sans: Inter, ui-sans-serif` are different strings to the CSSOM)
  and `calc(100% - 32px)` needs its spaces; and it **verifies by parsing both
  files in Chromium and diffing the rule lists**, not by diffing text. Text
  only proves the script did as it was told.
- **Bump the service worker on any CSS or JS change.** `service-worker.js`
  caches by version; raise `CACHE_NAME` *and* `ASSETS_CACHE` together. HTML is
  network-first, so skipping this hands returning visitors new markup with an
  old stylesheet — that is how every slideshow once rendered as a vertical
  column of photos. A hard refresh looks fine, so testing will not catch it.
  `styles.min.css` is also requested as `?v=N`; raise that when the file changes.
- **Site-wide changes** (nav, footer, tracking snippets, social links) must be
  applied across all 67 HTML pages — e.g. the HubSpot snippet is on all of them.
- **Photos:** run the `add-project-photos` skill rather than doing it by hand.
  Budgets are 1400px/180 KB full and 800px/80 KB for `-sm` thumbnails, and
  every photo needs both. Encode first, then write `srcset` width descriptors
  from the files on disk — writing them first and re-encoding after makes them
  lie. A `<source>` needs descriptors and `sizes`, or a `media` query; a bare
  one-URL srcset beats the `<img>` and pins the full-size file for everyone.
- **Brand red:** `--color-brand` (#E63946) fails AA as small text — 4.16:1 both
  on white and with white on it. Small text uses `--color-brand-hover`
  (#D62828), which passes ~4.96:1 either way. On near-black, the brighter
  `--metal` is the legible one. Keep #E63946 for fills and large text.
- **Never let a bot check refuse a lead.** Turnstile blocking the quote form
  cost roughly 25 days of submissions: the widget failed, no token was minted,
  and both the client gate and the server's 403 turned real customers away.
  Verification is advisory now — an unverified lead arrives with an
  `[unverified]` subject prefix and per-IP rate limits bound abuse instead.
  `/api/health-check` exists to catch a regression here.
- **A configured integration is not a working one.** `RESEND_API_KEY` being set
  says nothing about whether Resend will *accept* the send: an unverified From
  domain is refused at the API, `submit-quote` answers 502, and the owner's
  inbox stays empty while every page looks fine. Health checks here assert
  delivery, not configuration.
- **Graceful degradation** is the house style: reviews fall back to a static
  payload, chat degrades to phone/quote CTAs, and a quote the API cannot take
  falls back rather than vanishing. **A fallback nobody wired up is worse than
  none** — the quote forms carry no `action`, and for months `nativeFallback()`
  posted them to the static page they sit on, so every 500/502/503 deleted the
  lead in silence while the code claimed it went to Formspree. `quote-form.js`
  now submits natively only when the form has an `action` that leaves the page;
  otherwise it keeps the customer put and hands them their request back with a
  phone number and a prefilled email. To turn the Formspree net on, put
  `action="https://formspree.io/f/<id>"` on the five `#quote-form` forms.
  Related: `form.dispatchEvent(new Event('submit'))` runs listeners but never
  navigates — use `form.submit()` when you mean to post.
- **The work is buildings and glass. Not vehicles, screen doors or skylights.**
  No auto glass, windshields or vehicle glass — that boundary is already stated
  in the page copy and the JSON-LD descriptions. Add to it: **they do not do
  screen doors or skylights either.** The Google Business Profile listed screen
  door installation, screen door repair, skylight installation and skylight
  repair as services on 2026-10-20; those came from Google's suggested services
  for the "Glass shop" category, not from anything anyone claimed, and they
  were removed. The site has never mentioned either, and a grep for
  `screen.?door|skylight` across the repo should keep returning nothing.
  The trap is the direction of the fix. Someone comparing the profile against
  the site will find four services on Google with no page behind them and
  "complete" the site to match. That is backwards: the site is right and the
  listing was wrong. A service advertised but not performed books a visit
  nobody can carry out, and on a profile with five reviews a single one-star
  for wasting an afternoon is 17% of the rating.
- **The conversion here is a phone call, so count it.**
  `assets/js/call-tracking.js` is on all 65 pages that carry a `tel:` link,
  placed by `tools/add_call_tracking.py` (scope derived from the pages
  themselves — any page with a `tel:` link gets it — so a page that gains a
  phone link later is picked up by a re-run rather than missed). Five
  analytics systems were loaded and none measured a single call, which made
  "why am I not getting calls?" unanswerable: nobody could say whether the
  site produced zero or twenty. It reports a `source` (sticky mobile button,
  utility bar, header, hero, final CTA, footer, emergency band, content), not
  just that a tap happened. It obeys the same rule as the Turnstile gate that
  once refused real leads — **a measurement convenience must never stand
  between a customer and the business**: capture-phase, `passive`, no
  `preventDefault`, every send fire-and-forget inside its own `try/catch`, and
  with every analytics global missing the tap still dials. A tracking failure
  costs a data point; blocking a tap costs a job.
- **Motion layer.** `assets/css/motion.css` + `assets/js/motion.js`, loaded on
  all 65 real pages (not `emergency.html`, a redirect stub, nor the Google
  verification file). It is purely additive: it changes no colour, type,
  spacing or layout, and attaches to markup that already exists. The rule that
  makes it safe is the ordering — `motion.js` adds `.motion-ready` to `<html>`
  only after it has found its targets and confirmed IntersectionObserver, and
  every hiding rule in the CSS is scoped under that class. So with the script
  blocked, JS off, or reduced motion requested, nothing is ever hidden. There
  is also a 4s failsafe that reveals anything still hidden. Never write a
  `[data-rise]` rule that hides outside `.motion-ready`.
  Note `.section` sets `content-visibility: auto`, so scrolling straight to the
  bottom and then reading computed styles reports skipped sections as
  un-transitioned — that is a measurement artifact, not a bug. Check motion
  with `tools/motion-inview.cjs` (measures in-viewport, as a visitor sees it);
  `tools/motion-check.cjs` covers the JS-off and reduced-motion cases, where
  nothing may ever be hidden.

  **"Purely additive" was not true, and it cost every inner page its hero
  photograph.** `motion.css` lifted the content above its decorative wash with
  `.inner-hero > *, .final-cta > * { position: relative; z-index: 1 }`. That
  `*` also caught `.inner-hero-image`, which must stay `position: absolute` to
  fill the hero. Forced into flow it becomes a flex item of an
  `align-items: end` container whose only content is an absolutely positioned
  `<img>` — no in-flow content, so it computes 0×0, and the `<img>` sized
  `inset: 0` against it collapses too. All 58 `.inner-hero` pages downloaded
  their hero photo at `fetchpriority="high"` and painted nothing; the heroes
  looked like a deliberate flat-black design. `styles.css` carries the same
  stacking idea on `.section > *` and excludes the four image wrappers by name
  — `.hero-image`, `.hero-scrim`, `.emergency-image`, `.inner-hero-image`. The
  motion copy was written without them. **Keep the two exclusion lists
  identical.**

  Three things worth keeping from it. The bug lived inside
  `@media (prefers-reduced-motion: no-preference)`, so the hero rendered
  *correctly* for anyone who had asked for reduced motion — which is the one
  state a careless check is most likely to run in, so check all three.
  The wash itself had been designed against the bug it caused: its comment
  said these heroes are "already solid --ink", which was only true because the
  photo was not painting. And restoring the photo exposed a contrast failure
  that had been masked for as long as the bug existed — the scrim runs
  left-to-right, which works while the copy sits in the left half of a wide
  hero and fails below ~900px where the copy spans the width and every line
  ends over the 20%-alpha tail. Body copy measured 1.6:1 to 4.3:1 against AA's
  4.5 on ten pages at 390px. There is now a vertical scrim under
  `@media(max-width:900px)`. **Fixing a rendering bug can uncover a second
  problem that only existed in theory while nothing was drawn — re-run the
  contrast check after anything starts painting that was not painting before,
  and measure under the glyphs (`Range.getClientRects()`), not the element
  box: a block `<p>` spans the whole column while its text occupies half of
  it, and sampling the empty remainder reported nine false failures.**
- **Review markup must match what the page shows.** `data/reviews-baseline.js`
  holds the rating and count the JSON-LD declares, and both review endpoints
  read from it, so the visible block and the structured data agree even when
  the Places API is down. Before, the fallback returned 0/0 and the section
  collapsed to "temporarily unavailable" while the markup still claimed 76
  reviews — the mismatch Google's self-serving review policy targets. Review
  *text* is never faked; quotes render only when they come live from Google.
  `REVIEWS_BASELINE_RATING` / `REVIEWS_BASELINE_COUNT` override without a deploy.

  **The baseline itself must match the Google Business Profile, and on
  2026-10-20 it did not.** It read 4.4 / 76 while the profile had 5 reviews
  averaging 5.0 — 15x more reviews than exist, declared in `aggregateRating`
  on 54 pages, at a *lower* average than the real one. So the site was taking
  the policy risk and getting a worse number for it. Corrected to 5.0 / 5.

  The rule above was written about the fallback returning 0/0 while the markup
  claimed 76, and it fixed that. Nobody checked whether the 76 was real. The
  lesson worth keeping: making two numbers in the repo agree with each other
  proves nothing if neither was ever checked against the profile. **The profile
  is the source of truth for both numbers — verify against it, not against
  another copy in the repo.** Once a review count moves, it moves in
  `data/reviews-baseline.js` *and* in every page's JSON-LD, or the env
  overrides carry it without a deploy.

  That correction had to be made twice, which is the other lesson. The first
  pass walked `glob.glob('*.html')` and so fixed only the repo root, leaving
  4.4 / 76 in 136 blocks under `secondary-domain/` and in the prose of
  `llms.txt`, `llms-full.txt` and
  `docs/internal/citation-correction-request.md` — a number stated in English
  rather than in JSON, which a JSON-shaped search will never find. **Fix a
  published fact with a walk of the whole repo and two greps: one for the
  markup form, one for the prose form.** The only copies that keep the old
  numbers are the narrative ones in this file and in
  `data/reviews-baseline.js`, which exist to explain the correction.
- **The address is whatever the Google Business Profile says, to the letter.**
  `4949 N Loop 1604 W Bldg 5 Suite 501, San Antonio, TX 78249`. The site read
  `4949 N Loop 1604 West, Suite 501` until 2026-10-20 — missing `Bldg 5`, and
  `West` where the verified listing says `W`. That is a NAP inconsistency on
  every page, in `llms.txt`, and in `data/company-knowledge.js`, which is what
  the chat answers "where are you?" from. The profile is the verified record,
  so it wins; the site changes to match it, never the reverse. It appears 398
  times across 159 files including the non-deployed copies, so change it with
  a scripted replace over the whole repo and grep for leftovers afterwards.
  `docs/internal/citation-correction-request.md` matters as much as the pages
  do: it is the template for telling other directories the correct address, so
  a stale copy there propagates the error outward rather than just sitting
  there.
- **A city page's `Place` is the city; its `LocalBusiness` is the company.**
  `LocalBusiness` geo is the San Antonio office (29.5604, -98.5322) and must
  match `postalAddress`. `Place` geo is that city's own coordinates and must
  match its `hasMap`. Setting both to the office address looks like a NAP fix
  and is not: it leaves `Place` contradicting its own map link on 21 pages.
  `tools/fix_place_geo.py` restores `Place` geo from `hasMap`.
- **One hours declaration, site-wide.** Every page's `openingHoursSpecification`
  says 00:00–23:59, seven days, because the emergency line really is answered
  around the clock. `index.html` and `about.html` used to declare office hours
  instead, so the site told Google two different things about the same business
  `@id` on 54 pages versus 2. `tools/fix_hours_conflict.py` converges them and
  its verification pass reports how many distinct declarations exist — the
  answer should always be one. The Google Business Profile must match.

  **On 2026-10-20 it did not, and was fixed the same day.** The profile read
  "Open · Closes 5 PM" while all 75 `openingHoursSpecification` blocks said
  00:00–23:59. The profile even contradicted itself: its own description says
  emergency support runs 24/7 including weekends and holidays, directly below a
  field saying the business shuts at five. The cost was every "open now" search
  after 5 PM — the smashed storefront at 9 PM is the highest-intent call this
  business gets. The profile has since been set to Open 24 hours and the two
  now agree. (Set by the account owner and reported here; this container cannot
  reach Google to confirm it independently.)

  The method is the part to keep. The profile is edited by hand in Google and
  no script here can reach it, so **the check is to open the profile and read
  the hours**, not to run the tool and see one declaration. The tool proves the
  pages agree with each other, which is a different claim from agreeing with
  Google — the same gap that let a fabricated review count sit unchallenged
  until someone finally opened the profile and looked.

  **Do not "fix" the `contactPoint` hours in `index.html`.** They are the one
  place in the repo that says 08:00–17:00, so they look like the bug the rule
  above is about. They are not: `contactPoint` carries Mon–Fri 08:00–17:00 and
  Sat 09:00–13:00 for `customer service` and `"24/7"` for `emergency`, which is
  accurate and more precise than the blanket figure — the office is staffed
  business hours, the emergency line is answered around the clock. Both are
  true at once. `fix_hours_conflict.py` only reads `openingHoursSpecification`
  and is blind to `contactPoint`, which is why this needs saying rather than
  checking.
- **The forms and `validateQuote` state one contract.** Required is first name,
  email, service and consent — four fields, cut from eight on 2026-10-12. The
  forms used to demand `details` and `phone` that the server never wanted, and
  the server demanded last name and location that the forms could not usefully
  insist on; at ~0.3% conversion the friction was the cheaper half to fix.
  `tools/form-required-check.py` asserts the asterisks match the `required`
  attributes and both match the server, treating the planning checklist as its
  own shape via the same rule `quote-form.js` uses (`details || timeline`).
  Relaxing one side alone turns silent abandonment into a 422 after the customer
  has finished typing, which is worse than the problem.
- **Hero background video:** `assets/Videos/hero.mp4` serves as the autoplaying,
  muted, looped hero background on `index.html`. It must always be encoded with
  faststart (`-movflags +faststart`) and accompanied by static poster fallbacks
  (`hero-video-poster.webp` / `hero-video-poster.jpg`) so users on slow
  connections or with `prefers-reduced-motion` enabled see a clean, accessible frame.
- **Specification-Only Configurator (Option C):** The interactive glass configurator
  on `index.html` generates real-time architectural specifications without
  publishing estimated dollar amounts. Clicking "Get Custom Pricing for this Spec"
  navigates to `/request-quote` with service and specification pre-filled via URL
  parameters.
